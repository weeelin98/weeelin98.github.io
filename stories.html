A Curious Mind, a Controller, and a Few Unconventional Ideas

I’ve always had a habit of noticing things that could work a little better. Since childhood, spotting a need has usually led me to another question: could I find a different way to solve it? My first idea isn’t always the most conventional one, but exploring unexpected possibilities is often my favorite part.

As a Gemini, I like to joke that my mind rarely has just one tab open. There’s usually a project idea, a half-translated sentence, and a completely unrelated joke competing for attention.

Before I learned to write code, I spent seven years studying traditional Chinese painting and another year learning sketching. Painting taught me to look closely at small details while keeping the whole composition in mind. I learned to be patient with individual brushstrokes and to step back when I needed a clearer view.

I still work that way when I’m building a website, thinking through a game level, or organizing a project. I move between individual pieces and the whole experience, checking how they fit together.

I studied International Economics as an undergraduate, earned a master’s degree in Translation, and am now pursuing a master’s in Computer Science at Georgia Tech, focusing on artificial intelligence.

Economics encouraged me to think about systems and incentives. Translation trained me to pay attention to context, meaning, and perspective. With computer science, I can turn those ideas into things people can use.

My work spans project management for game development, translation, and full-stack development. During engineering internships in Hong Kong, I worked on data ingestion, semantic search, and cloud services. I enjoy understanding what happens behind the scenes, while also caring about the part of a product people touch.

Sometimes an everyday inconvenience stays on my mind until I start thinking about how to fix it. That’s part of what draws me to software and website development: I can work out what someone needs, try a few approaches, and build something they can use.

I’m fluent in three languages and currently learning Japanese and Spanish. Moving between languages has made me attentive to how differently people can express the same thought and how easily meaning can shift along the way. My empathy often leads me to consider several perspectives at once, while my sense of humor supplies a steady stream of unexpected connections. If I suddenly laugh in the middle of a quiet moment, a joke may have just finished translating itself in my head.

I’m a longtime Soulsborne player, with a particular fondness for Dark Souls III, Bloodborne, and Elden Ring. My wider gaming interests also include Elden Ring Nightreign and Phantom Blade Zero.

I especially enjoy studying level design: how a route encourages exploration, how an encounter changes the rhythm of a space, or how a shortcut brings you back somewhere familiar with a completely different understanding of it. I can spend as much time wondering why an area works as I do trying to survive it.

My interests reach well beyond Soulslikes. They include Split Fiction, Dead Island 2, Silent Hill 2, Assassin’s Creed Odyssey, Astro Bot, Resident Evil 4, Resident Evil Requiem, Ghost of Yōtei, Hades, Hades II, Pragmata, Crimson Desert, and Cyberpunk 2077.

I also love The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, Animal Crossing, Left 4 Dead, Stardew Valley, and Disco Elysium. There’s room in my life for difficult boss fights, complicated conversations, and the very serious responsibility of looking after a virtual farm.

Away from the screen, I love traveling, observing everyday life, and meeting fluffy animals. Travel gives me new places to explore and new habits to notice. Animals are much more efficient: they can usually claim my full attention immediately.

A conversation, something I notice while traveling, or a small frustration can give me an idea I want to follow. Sometimes it becomes a project or a sketch. Sometimes it just leaves me with another question.

There’s usually room for one more open tab.
